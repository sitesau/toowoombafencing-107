The useful first sketch is not a style mood board. It is the line on the ground: where the fence must sit, how far it runs, where it turns, how the block falls, and what already stands there. Old concrete, tight side access, a retaining wall, a corner block, pool zone rules or a shared boundary can change labour before any panel is chosen.

  • Note run length, corners, returns and changes in direction.
  • Flag existing posts, footings, rock, hard ground and machinery limits.
  • Say early if a neighbour, pool barrier, wall or corner sightline is involved.

Let the fence purpose narrow the shortlist

Different problems deserve different fences. A screen that must block sightlines is not the same brief as airflow near a courtyard, a compliant pool barrier, an acreage line, a school frontage or a patch after storm damage. Begin with verbs: block, reveal, allow, contain, secure, repair.

  • Privacy and street presentation: Colorbond is framed as a common low maintenance boundary option; timber can suit paling, lapped and capped, or picket looks.
  • Modern frontages: aluminium slats and screens suit courtyards where airflow and presentation matter.
  • Low upkeep panels: PVC is described as long life vinyl panel and picket fencing that does not need painting.
  • Pool zones: compliant glass and aluminium pool barriers are listed separately under Queensland pool safety standards.
  • Acreage and work sites: ringlock, strained wire, star picket and post and rail sit outside a suburban brief; chain wire, tubular steel and commercial grade fencing suit business premises and schools.

Ask quote questions that expose hidden work

A cheaper number can hide the parts that make a fence stand up. Ask each written quote to state materials, posts, gates, supports, removal, access assumptions and how stepped panels, corners, old concrete or hard ground are handled. If licensing may apply, ask whether the contractor holds a current QBCC licence with scope suited to the work.

  • Confirm the proposed boundary position before panels are selected.
  • Describe slope, direction changes, existing posts and footings.
  • State whether the aim is privacy, pool safety, street presence, rural use, security or repair.
  • Request the rule, licence scope or process that applies where pool safety or a neighbour issue is involved.

Two quotes are comparable only when they assume the same site truth.

Plan gates as structure, not an add on

Gates change posts, openings, clearances and hardware, so they belong in the first layout talk. A pedestrian gate, swing driveway gate, sliding gate or automated driveway gate can demand stronger supports and different clearance thinking. Solar powered openers are also named, so power access and daily use should be raised before the line is settled.

  • Ask where the opening will sit and how the gate will swing or slide.
  • Check whether adjoining sections need stronger supports.
  • On a frontage, weigh privacy, sightlines and screen style together.

A gate can reveal trouble in an otherwise simple run: poor drainage at an opening, a tight turn, shared driveway conflict or a post line that fights the boundary.

Repair or replace by failure pattern

Repair is realistic when damage is localised: a leaning post, broken rail or storm affected section while the rest of the run remains sound. Straight replacement can be more economical where failed sections dominate or patching would leave a poor finish. Describe the failure, not only the material.

  • Send photos, approximate run length and access notes.
  • Ask whether sound sections can remain.
  • Ask if new posts are required and whether old concrete or hard ground changes labour.

Those answers help separate a small repair, partial replacement and full new fence when comparing prices.

Common questions

Which fence material should I compare first for a low maintenance boundary? Compare Colorbond for a common low maintenance boundary option, then weigh timber for paling, lapped and capped or picket styles, aluminium slats for airflow and presentation, and PVC where long life vinyl panels and pickets that do not need painting are preferred.

What details make a fence quote more accurate? Give the suburb, run length, boundary position, slope, corners, returns, existing posts or concrete, access limits, neighbour or pool issues, and the purpose of the fence. Ask what is included for gates, supports, removal and hard ground.

When is repair enough instead of a new fence? Repair can fit when failure is localised, such as a leaning post, broken rail or storm damaged section while the rest of the run is sound. Replacement may fit when straight replacement is more economical or patching would leave a poor finish.
